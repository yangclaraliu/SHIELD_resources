## Voice and tone
- Scientific, human, global, just, expert — these are PATH's five brand attributes.
- Avoid jargon; define technical terms at first use even for specialist audiences.
- Avoid contractions in formal writing; acceptable in web copy or casual tone.
- PATH uses *we/our* in most materials; *it/its* when collaborating on joint publications.

## Capitalisation
- **Sentence case** for all headings and subheadings within a document (preferred over title case).
- **Title case** for cover page titles and subtitles (optional).
- Do not capitalise common nouns, even technical ones with abbreviations.
- Capitalise official job titles before or after a name; lowercase occupational descriptions.
- Capitalise Ministry of Health when referring to a specific ministry; lowercase when plural or generic.
- Capitalise Board of Directors and Executive Team; shortened forms are *board* and *ET*.
- Capitalise directional adjectives that are part of a proper name (South Africa, Southeast Asia); lowercase general directions (northern Africa, eastern Asia).
- Capitalise district, province, region, state when part of a formal name (Sunsari District); lowercase otherwise (district of Sunsari).
- Capitalise Phase when describing a clinical trial (Phase 3).
- Capitalise project only when part of the official project name.
- Capitalise Black (racial/ethnic adjective); lowercase brown and white.
- COVID-19: always all-caps with the hyphen; never Covid-19 or covid-19 in external communications.

## Abbreviations
- Only use an abbreviation if it appears at least twice after being defined.
- Define as: Full Term (ABBR). Do not define in main document titles.
- Re-define in long documents after an executive summary, and in later sections.
- No periods in academic degrees: MD, MPH, PhD.
- Use *an* before abbreviations starting with a vowel sound (an NGO).
- Do not add *the* before WHO (but use *the* before World Health Organization).
- No need to define: AIDS, COVID-19, DNA, HIV, RNA, SARS-CoV-2.
- Plural abbreviations: add *s* (NGOs). Exception: spell out *ministries of health* rather than MOHs.
- Abbreviations in figures/tables must be defined within the figure/table regardless of text definition.

## Numbers and measurements
- Spell out one through ten; use numerals for 11 and above.
- Always spell out numbers that begin a sentence.
- Use numerals for: percentages, measurements (weight, volume, distance), time in technical documents, ages, clinical trial phases.
- Use commas in numbers ≥1,000 (e.g., 10,000).
- Large numbers: use numerals + million/billion (10 million doses).
- Dates: **month day, year** (January 1, 2024). Use comma before and after year in midsentence. No comma when only month and year.
- Currency: specify type for international audiences (US$1,000 or USD 1,000; not $1,000).
- Percentages: use numerals + *percent* in narrative; % symbol acceptable in tables, figures, technical documents.
- Decimals in scientific/technical documents; fractions as words in narrative (two-thirds).
- Temperature: no space between numeral, °, and scale abbreviation (37°C).
- Ranges in text: *from … to*, *from … through*, *between … and*. En dash in tables/figures (0–6 months).
- Use *more than* / *fewer than* in formal/technical writing (not *over* / *under* for quantities).
- Use *less than* for degree, value, time, or distance; *fewer than* for count.

## Punctuation
- **Serial (Oxford) comma**: always use after penultimate item in a list of three or more.
- **Em dash** (—): no spaces before or after. Use to set off elements mid-sentence or at end.
- **En dash** (–): use for number/date ranges, and in compound adjectives when one element is open (health care–related).
- **Hyphen**: compound adjective before noun (HIV-positive adults); not needed predicatively (adults were HIV positive). Do not hyphenate *-ly* adverbs.
- **Colon**: use after independent clause to introduce a list or for emphasis; lowercase what follows unless it begins an independent clause.
- **Semicolons**: recommended (not required) in lists of three or more items when any item contains an internal comma.
- **Apostrophe**: add *'s* for possessives not ending in *s* (Zambia's, WHO's); add *'* only for words ending in *s* (the virus', the midwives').
- **Quotation marks** (American style): periods and commas inside; all other punctuation outside.
- One space after all punctuation marks. No spaces around hyphens or dashes.
- *e.g.* and *i.e.*: inside parentheses only, with periods, followed by a comma.
- Ampersand (&): only in official names or standard abbreviations (M&E, R&D).

## Word choice — DEI
- Do not use *beneficiary*; prefer *people*, *communities*, *clients*, *partners*, *end users*.
- Do not use *developing country*; prefer specific country names, or *low-income*, *low-resource* only when a generalisation is unavoidable.
- Do not use *on the ground* or *in the field* — be specific about location and context.
- Avoid *empower*; use specific verbs: equip, inform, educate, train.
- Use *woman/women* (not *female*) as noun and adjective, except when biological sex is specifically relevant (e.g., clinical trial participants).
- Use gender-neutral language (singular *they*; *chair* not *chairman*).
- Use identified pronouns for known individuals; singular *they* for unknown/nonspecific.
- At risk / vulnerable: only with clear, specific context — name what the group is at risk of.

## References (modified AMA style)
- Preferred system: superscript numerals in order of citation.
- Authors: surname + initials, no periods, no spaces between initials. First three authors + *et al.* if more than six.
- Journal titles: write out in full (not Index Medicus abbreviations).
- Journal article titles: sentence case; do not capitalise after colon unless proper noun.
- Book/standalone titles: title case, italicised.
- DOI preferred over URL when available; do not include both.
- Personal communications: cite in text only (not in reference list); obtain written permission.
- Page ranges: all digits (465–469, not 465–9); use en dash.

## Document structure (formal reports)
Order of elements: front cover → inside front cover (contact info, copyright, suggested citation, disclaimers) → title page → table of contents → acknowledgments → abbreviations list → foreword/preface → executive summary → body → references → appendixes/annexes.

- Copyright: © [Year] PATH. All rights reserved.
- Suggested citation format: Author Initials Surname. *Title in Title Case*. PATH; Year.
- Appendixes: labelled with letters (Appendix A). Annexes are standalone previously published documents.
- Executive summary: max two pages; a summary of the whole document, not an introduction.

## Formatting (Word documents)

**Body text**
- Font: 10pt Arial, 0pt before, 6pt after, line spacing multiple 1.2.
- Text alignment: left-aligned, ragged right margin.

**Headings**
- Sentence case throughout.
- Five levels:
  - Section title: Arial 18pt red regular, 0pt before, 18pt after, line spacing 1.2
  - Heading 1: Arial 18pt bold, 24pt before, 6pt after, line spacing 1.2
  - Heading 2: Arial 14pt bold, 12pt before, 6pt after, line spacing 1.2
  - Heading 3: Arial 11pt bold, 12pt before, 6pt after, line spacing 1.2
  - Heading 4: Arial 10pt bold, 12pt before, 6pt after, line spacing 1.2

**Lists**
- Left-aligned with preceding text.
- Introduced by a colon.
- Each item: sentence case, grammatically parallel, ends with full stop (except one- or two-word items).

**Figures and tables**
- Numbered sequentially in separate sequences (Figure 1, Table 1).
- Titles above, sentence case, ending with full stop.
- Introduce in text before they appear.
- Abbreviations defined within figure/table regardless of text definition.

**Other**
- Italics for: words referred to as words, non-English terms not in Merriam-Webster, genus/species names, titles of published works.
- Photo credits required (legal obligation): format as *PATH* or *PATH/Photographer*.
- Hyperlinks: remove underline for print documents.
- Turn off Word hyphenation (Layout > Hyphenation > None).
- Page numbers: consecutive; front matter may use Roman numerals (i, ii, iii).
- One space after all punctuation; no spaces around hyphens or dashes.
